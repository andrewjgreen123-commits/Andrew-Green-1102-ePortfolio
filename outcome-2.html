ENC 1102 · LEARNING OUTCOME 02

Information Literacy

Finding, evaluating, and using credible and relevant information to support research.

ARTIFACT 01

Annotated Bibliography

My annotated bibliography helped me practice information literacy by finding and evaluating sources related to how audience, purpose, and context influence writing. I looked for research that approached my question from different angles, including studies about audience awareness, writing strategies, and student motivation. I considered each source’s credibility, relevance to my research question, and limitations. For example, some studies focused on high-school students or very small groups, so I had to consider how directly their findings applied to college students.

Reflection: Evaluating Information

One challenge was finding sources that were useful without expecting every study to match my research question exactly. Writing the annotations made me look beyond simply collecting information. I had to explain what each source contributed, recognize its limitations, and connect it to other research. I also needed to represent the authors’ findings fairly rather than treating their conclusions as proof of something they had not studied. This assignment helped me understand that credible research can still have limitations, and that those limitations matter when deciding how to use a source. The annotated bibliography gave me a stronger foundation for my own research and helped me become more careful about how I find, evaluate, and use information.

ARTIFACT 02

Researching Possible Secondary Sources

Finding secondary sources helped me understand how researchers have studied writing for different audiences and purposes. I looked for sources that discussed writing styles, genres, motivation, and audience expectations. Instead of including information just because it was related to writing, I had to consider whether each source was relevant to my research question and whether its findings could help me understand my topic. This process helped me build a foundation for my project and identify ideas I could explore through my own survey.

Reflection: Finding and Evaluating Information

My research process involved looking at different perspectives and deciding what each source could contribute. I considered credibility by looking at where the research was published, and I evaluated relevance by connecting each source to my question about audience, purpose, and motivation. One challenge was that some studies focused on specific groups, such as high-school students or writers with particular backgrounds, rather than college students in general. I learned that this did not automatically make a source useless, but it meant I needed to be careful about how I applied its findings. I also needed to represent the researchers' ideas fairly and recognize the limits of their conclusions. This artifact shows my growth in information literacy because I learned to do more than collect sources: I had to evaluate, compare, and use information responsibly to support my own research.

What I Learned from Finding Sources

Looking through these sources helped me see that writing is affected by several connected factors. Audience and genre help explain how writing changes between situations, while research on motivation and self-efficacy offers other ways to think about students' writing choices. Collecting these sources gave me different perspectives to consider while developing my own research project.
