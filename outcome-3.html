Project 1: Draft 1 was my first attempt at bringing together the research I had been working on throughout the semester into a complete investigative research paper. The assignment required me to develop an introduction, explain my research methods, present my findings, and begin analyzing the evidence I had collected. I also had to incorporate secondary sources to help establish the existing conversation around my topic. My goal was to organize my research into a structure that would communicate my ideas clearly to an academic audience while developing a claim that responded to my research question.

Reflection: Developing an Academic Research Paper

Writing this first draft helped me understand that producing a research paper involves more than just gathering information and putting it together. I had to make decisions about how to organize my ideas, introduce my topic, connect different sources, and present evidence in a way that supported my overall argument. I also learned that research writing has certain expectations and conventions, but there is still room to make choices about how information is presented. Since this was an early draft, I knew that some parts would need improvement, but it gave me an opportunity to see how all of my research could come together as one piece of writing. This artifact demonstrates my progress toward Research Genre Production because I was learning to navigate the structure and expectations of an academic research paper.

CARS Model Introduction

The CARS Model Introduction assignment helped me practice structuring an academic research introduction using three main steps: establishing a research territory, identifying a gap in existing research, and explaining how my own research would address that gap. Using sources from my annotated bibliography, I had to summarize the existing conversation surrounding how college students change their writing for different audiences. I then identified areas that needed further investigation and introduced my own research question. This exercise gave me a structure to follow when introducing my topic and explaining why my research was worth exploring.

Reflection: Learning to Structure Research Introductions

This assignment helped me understand how academic research introductions do more than simply introduce a topic. They establish what is already known, identify what is missing, and explain why a new study is worth conducting. I found the idea of identifying a gap especially useful because it pushed me to think about what my research could contribute instead of just repeating information from other sources. It also helped me connect my sources to my own research question and create a clearer direction for my paper. I later used the CARS model when developing the introduction for my first research paper draft. This artifact demonstrates my progress toward Research Genre Production because I learned how to use an established academic writing structure to communicate the purpose and relevance of my research.
