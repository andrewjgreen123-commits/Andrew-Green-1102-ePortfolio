The Reading Response: Analyzing Primary Data assignment focused on understanding how to interpret and organize information collected through primary research. Using the readings by Denny and Clark and Wardle and Downs, I explored the process of coding qualitative data, identifying patterns, and considering ethical responsibilities when analyzing research. I also had to explain how I planned to apply these methods to my own research project. This required me to think about how I would organize student responses, identify meaningful similarities and differences, and make sure I represented the information accurately.

Reflection: Understanding and Interpreting Research Data

This assignment helped me understand that collecting research data is only one part of the research process. I also needed to know how to interpret that information and identify patterns that could help answer my research question. Learning about coding showed me how individual responses can be organized into categories to make larger patterns easier to recognize. I also became more aware of the ethical responsibility involved in interpreting other people's responses. It is important not to change the meaning of someone's answer or make conclusions that the evidence does not support. This became especially relevant to my own project because I was researching how students change their writing depending on their audience. This artifact demonstrates my progress toward Contributing Knowledge because it helped me understand how to move from simply collecting information to analyzing evidence and using it to develop meaningful conclusions.

Project 1: Draft 2 was a revised version of my investigative research paper that incorporated feedback from peer review and the additional research I had completed. One of the biggest differences between this draft and Draft 1 was that I had collected enough primary data to include actual student responses in my paper. This allowed me to move beyond discussing my research plans and begin presenting and analyzing the information I had gathered. I also had to code my primary data, identify patterns in the responses, and connect those findings to my secondary sources. Along with adding this research, I made revisions to the organization, explanations, and overall presentation of my paper based on feedback.

Reflection: Turning Research into Findings

This draft was an important point in my research process because I finally had enough primary data to support my ideas with actual evidence. Unlike my first draft, where I was still developing my argument and organizing my research, I could now analyze student responses and use them to draw conclusions about how college students change their writing for different audiences. Coding the responses helped me identify patterns and compare the different reasons students gave for changing their writing. I also had to consider how those findings connected to the ideas presented in my secondary sources. Incorporating peer feedback helped me improve how I communicated these findings and supported my overall argument. This artifact demonstrates my progress toward Contributing Knowledge because I moved beyond summarizing existing research and began using my own collected evidence to contribute to the conversation surrounding my research topic.
